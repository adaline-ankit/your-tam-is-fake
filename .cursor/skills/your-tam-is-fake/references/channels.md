# Channels

Load when the plan names a channel, or when it names five.

## The constraint nobody likes

**One channel gets you to your first $10M.** Not five. Companies that list six channels in a plan are describing a wish, because each channel has its own competency curve, its own creative cadence, and its own measurement discipline. Running six badly produces less than one well.

The strategic question is not "which channels exist" but **"which single channel could plausibly carry the whole plan, and what would prove it in 30 days?"**

## Channel fit by ACV

| Channel | ACV floor | Reality check |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| Content / SEO | Any | 6–12 months to compound. Now competing against unlimited generated content, so only differentiated-data or genuine-expertise content clears the bar |
| Paid search | ~$3K | Works only where high-intent keywords exist. Check whether anyone is searching before budgeting — for new categories, nobody is |
| Paid social | ~$1K | Needs sharp creative iteration and a targetable ICP. Expensive learning phase |
| Community | Any | Slowest, most durable, cannot be bought. Requires actual participation |
| Outbound | ~$15K | List quality dominates everything. Reply rates falling industry-wide |
| Partner / channel | ~$25K | Amplifies existing fit, never creates it. Partners sell what is easy |
| Marketplaces (cloud, app stores) | ~$10K | Underused for infrastructure. Buyers spend committed cloud budget, which shortcuts procurement entirely |
| Events / field | ~$50K | High cost per meeting, high conversion. Only above enterprise ACV |
| Product-led / viral | < $5K | Needs a genuine sideways spread mechanic, not a referral coupon |
| Founder-led / network | Any | Real, effective, and **does not scale**. Never mistake it for a channel |

## The "why now" of channel decay

Channels have arbitrage windows. Being early to one is worth more than being good at a saturated one — early Facebook ads, early SEO, early cold email, early developer content all produced outsized returns that later disappeared. The window closes when acquisition cost rises to the point where only the best-funded player can pay.

So ask: **is this channel currently cheap for a specific structural reason?** A new platform, a new format, an audience that has not been mined, a distribution surface that just opened. If the answer is "no, but we will execute better," expect to pay market rate for attention, and budget accordingly.

## Attribution honesty

Last-touch attribution flatters paid channels and steals credit from content, community, and word of mouth. Before scaling spend based on attributed CAC, ask new customers a single open question: *how did you first hear about us?* The gap between the self-reported answer and the dashboard is usually the finding — and it usually means the channel getting the budget is not the channel doing the work.

## Testing discipline

Every channel test declares, in writing, **before it launches**:

- budget and duration
- the single metric that decides it
- the threshold that means continue, and the threshold that means stop
- what it would take to be wrong

Without a pre-declared threshold, every result is interpreted as encouraging, because that is what humans do with ambiguous data they are emotionally invested in. That is how companies spend two years on a channel that never worked.
