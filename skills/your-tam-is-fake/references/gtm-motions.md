# GTM motions — matching the machine to the math

Load when a motion is proposed, or when ACV and headcount appear in the same conversation.

## The governing constraint

**ACV determines the motion.** Not founder preference, not what worked at their last company, not what the category leader does at 100x the scale.

A motion has a cost floor. If the cost of acquiring a customer through that motion exceeds what the customer is worth, no amount of execution excellence saves it. This is arithmetic, and it is where most GTM plans die.

| ACV band | Viable motions | Structurally impossible |
|----------|----------------|-------------------------|
| **< $1K** | Self-serve, PLG, community, content, marketplace | Any human touch pre-sale |
| **$1K–$10K** | PLG + assisted onboarding, inside sales on inbound only | Outbound-led field sales |
| **$10K–$50K** | Inside sales, PLG + sales-assist, partner channel | Enterprise field sales with SEs |
| **$50K–$250K** | Inside/mid-market sales with SE support, outbound viable | Pure self-serve |
| **> $250K** | Enterprise field sales, partners, executive-led | Self-serve, most paid acquisition |

**The classic fatal mismatch:** $8K ACV with a two-rep outbound team. A rep costs $150K+ fully loaded and closes maybe 30 deals a year at that price point → $240K revenue against $150K cost, before marketing, management, tooling, or the ramp period. It does not work, and it does not work *harder* with better reps.

The second classic: a $150K enterprise product with a self-serve signup and no way to talk to a human. Security review alone requires a person.

## The motions

### Product-led (PLG)
**Works when** value lands in one session without a human, the user can adopt without permission, and usage naturally spreads sideways.
**Requires** a genuinely fast time-to-value, transparent pricing, and a usage-based expansion path.
**Fails when** setup needs data migration or admin rights, value only appears at team scale, or the buyer is not the user.
**The trap:** free-tier costs scaling faster than conversion. Model free-tier COGS explicitly; AI-heavy products have destroyed themselves here, because inference is a real marginal cost and storage was not.

### Sales-led
**Works when** ACV supports headcount, the buying committee has 3+ people, and the problem is expensive enough to justify a process.
**Requires** a repeatable qualification framework, a real ramp plan, and pipeline coverage of 3–4x quota.
**Fails when** the founder is the only person who can close — that is not a motion, it is a personality. Test: has anyone other than a founder closed a deal at full price?

### Community-led
**Works when** practitioners have shared identity and gather somewhere already.
**Requires** patience measured in quarters and genuine participation. Extractive community strategy is visible immediately and permanently.
**Fails when** the "community" is a Slack the company created and nobody posts in.

### Partner / channel-led
**Works when** someone already has the trust and the account relationship, and your product makes their core offering more valuable.
**Requires** clear margin for the partner and near-zero implementation friction. Partners sell what is easy, and they never sell your product harder than their own.
**Fails when** used to compensate for weak direct demand. Channel amplifies product-market fit; it does not create it.

### Outbound
**Works when** the ICP is precisely identifiable, the trigger is observable from outside, and ACV clears $15K.
**Requires** list quality above all else. Bad list, perfect copy, zero pipeline.
**Fails when** the ICP is a vibe. Also increasingly fails on volume alone — reply rates have collapsed under AI-generated sequences, which cuts both ways for anyone selling AI-generated sequences.

## Efficiency check

Before recommending scale, verify:

- **CAC payback** — see `pricing-and-unit-economics.md`. Payback beyond the ACV-band norm means the motion is broken, not underfunded.
- **Founder-independence** — has a non-founder closed at list price?
- **Repeatability** — do the last 10 wins share an ICP and a trigger, or are they 10 unrelated stories? Ten unrelated stories means no motion exists yet, and hiring reps will convert cash into nothing.
- **Retention first** — scaling acquisition on top of leaky retention is buying customers to lose them faster. Fix NRR before touching spend.

## The sequencing error

Hiring a VP of Sales to *find* product-market fit. A VP of Sales scales a motion that already works; they cannot invent one. If founders cannot yet describe repeatably who buys and why, a VP hire burns 3–4 quarters and a lot of goodwill, and the post-mortem always blames the VP.

Same class of error: hiring a demand-gen lead before knowing which channel converts, and building a partner program before direct sales is repeatable.
