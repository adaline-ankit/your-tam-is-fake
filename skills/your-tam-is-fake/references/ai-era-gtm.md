# GTM in the AI era

Load when the plan involves content, SEO, discovery, attribution, outbound at volume, or any assumption inherited from a pre-2024 playbook.

**Why this file exists:** most GTM advice in circulation was written for a buyer who searched, clicked, and downloaded. That buyer is now in the minority. A plan built on the old mechanics will look competent and underperform, and the failure will be misdiagnosed as execution.

All figures here are [T2] with a 2025–2026 vintage. **Re-verify before quoting** — this is the fastest-moving material in the skill.

## What actually changed

- **~94% of B2B buyers** used generative AI somewhere in their most recent purchase process; a growing share **start** in a chatbot rather than a search engine.
- **Shortlists shrank to ~2.5 vendors**, down from ~3.2. And they form **before you know the buyer exists.**
- **Zero-click rates ~83%** when AI overviews appear. The answer is delivered; the click is not.
- **AI chat now shapes ~54% of shortlists** — reported as the single biggest influence.
- Buyers complete **4–5 pieces of independent research** before contacting sales.

## The strategic consequence

The old model was *capture demand at the moment of search*. That moment is being absorbed by systems that answer without sending traffic.

The shift: **you are no longer optimising to be clicked. You are optimising to be cited.**

Two hard implications:

1. **Traffic is a broken success metric.** A plan whose leading indicator is sessions is measuring a number that can fall while pipeline rises. Measure citations, branded search volume, and self-reported attribution instead.
2. **If you are not in the training and retrieval corpus, you are not on the shortlist** — and you will never see the loss, because there was no click to lose. This is the most dangerous property of the new funnel: the failure is invisible.

## What earns citations

Ranked by leverage, and note that the top three are all things AI cannot manufacture:

1. **Proprietary data and original research.** Numbers that exist nowhere else must be cited by name. This is now the highest-ROI content asset in B2B, and it is one of very few that a competitor cannot generate cheaply.
2. **Structured, extractable answers.** Clear question-shaped headings, direct answers in the first sentence, tables, defined terms. Formats that are easy to lift are the formats that get lifted.
3. **Third-party corroboration.** Review sites, comparison pages, community threads, directories. Retrieval systems weight consensus across sources — so your presence in *other people's* content matters more than your own site.
4. **Being the named default in your category.** Circular but real: the frame you own is the frame you get retrieved for. This raises the value of narrow positioning considerably.
5. **Undifferentiated volume content.** Now close to worthless. Generated content competing with generated content, in a channel that no longer sends clicks.

Practical GTM consequence: the content budget should move from volume toward **one defensible data asset plus systematic third-party presence.** If the plan says "publish 20 blog posts a month," that is a 2019 plan.

## Attribution is broken, and here is the honest replacement

The measurement gap is not marginal. Refine Labs measured a **90-percentage-point gap** between software-reported and customer self-reported attribution for web search across a 12-month, 620-conversion, $21.5M ARR study; **99%** of self-reported responses differed from last-touch. Their own reported figure: **97% of net-new ARR attributed to dark social** in 2022.

Take the direction, not the decimals — these are one firm's numbers with an interest in the conclusion. But the direction is corroborated everywhere and the mechanism is obvious: the influential interactions happen in private Slacks, DMs, podcasts, Reddit threads, and peer conversations that no tracker can see.

**The replacement stack:**

1. **Self-reported attribution** — one open field at conversion: *"How did you first hear about us?"* Highest-signal, cheapest, most-skipped instrument in B2B marketing.
2. **Incrementality testing** — turn a channel off in a region and observe. The only method that establishes causation rather than correlation.
3. **Marketing mix modelling** — for companies with enough spend and history to support it.
4. **Signal capture** — surveys, brand-lift measurement, community listening.

**What to do with last-touch data:** use it for optimisation *within* a channel, never for budget allocation *between* channels. Last-touch systematically over-credits the final paid touch and steals credit from the community, content, and word-of-mouth that created the demand — so budgeting on it defunds exactly what is working. If a plan reallocates spend based on dashboard CAC without a self-reported cross-check, say so plainly; it is the most common expensive mistake in the file.

## Outbound in an AI-saturated channel

Generated sequences at volume have compressed reply rates industry-wide. Consequences:

- **Volume is no longer a strategy.** 10,000 generated emails now underperform 100 researched ones, and they burn domain reputation on the way.
- **The externality problem.** Anyone *selling* AI outbound tooling is selling into a channel their own product degrades. Every customer they win makes the channel slightly worse for every other customer. Name this when it applies — it is a structural issue with no product fix.
- **Trigger-based beats persona-based.** An observable event — a funding round, a compliance date, a new exec, a migration, a job posting — earns a reply. A persona match does not.
- **Cheap channels are moving toward warm surfaces:** communities, partner referrals, customer networks, and the founder's own audience.

## Pricing and margin under AI

Two things the pre-2024 playbook gets wrong, both covered further in `pricing-and-unit-economics.md`:

- **Per-seat pricing on an automation product is self-defeating.** If you replace work, seats fall as you succeed. The buyer works this out before you do and it becomes their renewal leverage.
- **Gross margin is no longer an assumption.** Inference is a real marginal cost. A company modelling 80% margins on an inference-heavy product with an open free tier has an unmodelled liability that grows with adoption, and it surfaces during diligence.

## Verdict language

When a plan rests on pre-2024 mechanics, name the specific mechanic rather than gesturing at "AI changed things":

> Your demand-gen plan assumes a buyer who searches and clicks. ~83% of AI-overview searches now end without a click, and AI chat shapes over half of shortlists. Your content plan is optimised to be *found*; it needs to be optimised to be *cited* — and you have no proprietary data asset, which is the only durable way to be cited. That is the gap, and it is a 2-quarter fix, not a 2-week one.
