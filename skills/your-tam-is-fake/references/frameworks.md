## How founders actually find and judge ideas

Worth knowing because it tells you what kind of scrutiny an idea can bear.

**Notice, don't invent.** Paul Graham's core claim: ideas invented deliberately are "plausible-sounding but hollow." The good ones are noticed by someone at an unusual vantage point. **Strategic use:** ask where the founder's unfair vantage point is. If they noticed this from the inside of the problem, demand-side evidence is usually already in their head and you should extract it. If they invented it from a trend report, the demand evidence does not exist yet and W2 research is the whole job.

**Schlep blindness.** Founders unconsciously avoid ideas requiring tedious, unpleasant work — banking integrations, compliance, data cleanup, procurement. Graham's example is Stripe: obvious, painful, avoided for years because the work was miserable. **Strategic use:** a schlep is the most durable moat available to a startup, because well-funded competitors avoid it for the same reasons. When an idea's main objection is "this is a grind," that is often the bull case, not the bear case. Say so.

**Do things that don't scale.** Manual recruitment of early users, concierge delivery, unreasonable hand-holding. **Strategic use:** this is correct early *and* it is the most common source of a false GTM signal. Founder-led heroics prove the value proposition; they prove nothing about the motion. Both halves are true and both must be said.

**Repeatability, not revenue, is the readiness signal.** The gate for hiring the first rep is being able to complete: *"I can sell this when I talk to [X person] with [Y problem]."* A founder at $2M ARR with chaotic conversion rates cannot hire successfully; one at $500K with clean rates can. By deal 5, an objection from deal 1 should have a written answer. By deal 10, discovery should surface no new questions. **Strategic use:** this is the single best test for the "should we hire a VP of Sales" question, and it is a test on process, not on the P&L.

## Framework selection errors to catch

- **MEDDPICC on a $5K deal.** Qualification overhead has to be proportionate to deal size. See `sales-process.md`.
- **Category design with no capital.** Creating a category means funding market education for years. It is an enormous, occasionally correct bet — the category leader captures roughly 76% of category market cap — but it is the wrong default and a terrible seed-stage default.
- **PLG frameworks on a product needing an admin, a migration, and a security review.** The framework will produce clean-looking output that describes a business that cannot exist.
- **Sean Ellis 40% on a market of twelve buyers.** The survey needs a real sample. At low N, use revealed behaviour: renewals, expansion, usage depth.
- **Any framework applied before demand evidence exists.** Sequence matters: evidence, then structure. A framework filled from imagination is a deck, not an analysis.
