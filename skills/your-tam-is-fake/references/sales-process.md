# Sales process, capacity, and the revenue plan

Load when the plan involves reps, quotas, a revenue target, or an enterprise deal cycle.

## The bowtie, not the funnel

The traditional funnel ends at "closed won," which for a recurring-revenue business is roughly the midpoint of the value. Winning by Design's bowtie model extends it symmetrically: acquisition narrows to the deal, then **widens again** through onboarding, adoption, retention, and expansion.

```
   awareness                                          expansion
      \                                                   /
       \   education    →  CLOSED  →   onboarded          /
        \  selection        WON       impact              /
         \___________________/\______________________ ___/
```

Why it matters strategically: it forces the post-sale half onto the same instrumented footing as the pre-sale half. A company measuring only the left side will optimise CAC while NRR quietly decays, and will not see it until renewal season. Given that a new logo costs 3–5x an expansion and expansion closes far faster, the right half is usually where the cheapest growth is sitting.

**SPICED** is the companion conversation model — Situation, Pain, Impact, Critical Event, Decision — used across marketing, sales, and CS so the same facts travel with the account instead of being re-discovered at every handoff. The **Critical Event** is the piece most teams omit and the one that governs timing: without a dated event forcing a decision, deals slip indefinitely and forecast accuracy collapses.

## Qualification: match the framework to the deal size

Qualification overhead must be proportionate. Over-heavy qualification on small deals destroys velocity; under-heavy on large deals produces a forecast made of hope.

| Framework | Fits | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **BANT** (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) | Sub-$25K, 1–2 stakeholders, <30-day cycle | Built by IBM in the 1950s for single decision-makers. Fine as an early filter, insufficient for committees |
| **MEDDIC** (Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identify pain, Champion) | ~$25K–$100K, 3–5 stakeholders, 30–90 days | Asks the better question: who inside the account sells this when you are not in the room? |
| **MEDDPICC** (+ Paper process, Competition) | $100K+, 6+ stakeholders, 90+ days, formal procurement | Paper process earns its keep the moment legal and security are involved |

Distinction worth holding: **methodology** (SPIN, Challenger, Solution Selling) is how you run the conversation. **Qualification** (BANT, MEDDIC) is how you decide whether to keep investing. MEDDIC does not teach discovery; it tells you which deals deserve more of your quarter.

## The buying committee is the actual product constraint

Current reality for enterprise B2B:

- Buying groups run **5–16 people across up to four functions**; the median for $100K+ technology purchases is around **11 stakeholders** [T2, Gartner].
- **CFO gating** is now common above roughly $50K.
- **Security questionnaires arrive ~47 days earlier** in the cycle than in 2021 [T2] — security and procurement now enter in the first third of the evaluation, not at contract review.
- **77% of buyers** describe their most recent purchase as very complex or difficult [T2].
- Median mid-market/enterprise cycle sits around **4–5 months**, up 20–30% from 2021. Above $500K ACV, **12–18 months**.

Three consequences a GTM plan must address explicitly, or it is not a plan:

1. **You are not selling to a person.** Every stakeholder has a different pain and a different veto. Map who can say no — that list is longer than the list who can say yes.
2. **SOC 2 is GTM infrastructure, not compliance overhead.** Without it, a whole segment cannot buy you regardless of product quality, and the delay lands mid-cycle where it kills momentum.
3. **Cycle length must be in the revenue model.** A plan assuming 60-day cycles in a segment that takes 150 days is not aggressive, it is arithmetically wrong, and it will miss by a quarter minimum.

## Capacity model — the part that catches fantasy plans

Two equations. Every revenue plan must reconcile to them.

```
Productive capacity = ramped reps × quota × average attainment

Reps needed = (target − current productive capacity) ÷ (quota × expected attainment)
```

The word doing the work is **ramped**. A rep hired in month 10 contributes almost nothing this year.

Benchmarks to plug in [T2, re-verify]:

| Segment | Ramp | Productive ratio |
|---|---|---|
| SMB | 5–6 months | ~70% |
| Commercial | 7–9 months | ~67% |
| Enterprise | 10–12 months | ~63% |
| Strategic | 13–18 months | ~60% |

**Quota attainment:** ~70% median, 80%+ top quartile, under 55% bottom quartile.

The illustrative consequence: a 40-rep team with ~5.7-month average ramp and 30% annual attrition is **not** 40 quotas of capacity — it is closer to **22–25 productive quotas** [T2]. Plans built on headcount rather than *ramped, attaining* headcount miss by 40% or more, and they miss in a way that looks like a sales-execution failure rather than a planning failure.

Two rules when auditing a plan:

- **Attrition belongs in the model.** 25–30% annual is normal. A plan with zero attrition is not a plan.
- **Reconcile both directions.** Top-down target against bottom-up capacity. When they disagree, the gap is the finding — and the gap is where the year is actually lost.

## Sales velocity

```
Velocity = (opportunities × win rate × ACV) ÷ cycle length
```

Useful because it exposes which lever is cheapest. Founders reflexively pull on *opportunities* — more pipeline — when **cycle length** and **win rate** are usually more tractable and always cheaper. Halving the cycle doubles velocity with no new pipeline and no new headcount.

Highest-leverage cycle reducers, in rough order: pre-empting security review (SOC 2, a completed questionnaire, a trust page), a narrower ICP so discovery is shorter, a champion enablement kit for internal selling, and pricing that fits an existing budget line rather than requiring new budget approval.

## Pipeline coverage

3–4x quota in the current period for a functioning team. Below 3x, the quarter is already lost and no amount of activity fixes it in time.

Coverage is also the fastest way to detect fiction: if coverage looks healthy but win rates are collapsing, the pipeline contains deals that were never qualified. That is a qualification problem being reported as a pipeline problem, and adding pipeline makes it worse.
