The job-to-be-done is real but you've aimed the product at the cheap half of it. Writing the proposal is not what's hard for a freelance designer — *pricing* it and *getting the lead* are — and the writing half is already free inside Upwork, bundled in Bonsai/HoneyBook, and 90% solved by the ChatGPT tab they already pay for.

### THE NUMBER

Your number: $1.5T, "a tiny slice is huge."

That $1.5T is **freelancer earnings** — labor income paid *to* independent knowledge workers in 2024, ~20M people [T2, [Upwork Future Workforce Index, Apr 2025](https://investors.upwork.com/news-releases/news-release-details/upwork-study-finds-1-4-us-skilled-knowledge-workers-now-work)]. It is their revenue, not their software budget. You cannot sell into it any more than a payroll vendor can sell into US wages. Freelancers spend roughly 1–3% of revenue on *all* software [T3, aggregator survey] — every tool, Adobe included.

That's the bad kind of agreement.

Your figure is **~1,000,000× my base.**

### STRONGEST CASE FOR IT

Not the one you made. The proposal is the single highest-leverage document in a freelance designer's business, because it is where the price gets set — and designers systematically underprice, because they cannot articulate scope or value under time pressure. A product that ingests the discovery-call transcript, emits a scoped three-tier proposal with a value narrative and a defensible number, tracks views and acceptance, and then tells the designer *what pricing actually wins across the network* is not a writing tool. It's **pricing intelligence with a document as the delivery mechanism.**

That version has a measurable outcome (average project value, win rate), a moat that compounds with usage (nobody else holds the win/loss corpus for design projects), and a price ceiling set by value rather than by comparison to ChatGPT. Move a designer from $4K to $5.5K average project and $29/month is a rounding error — you can charge $99, or take a percentage.

### WHAT HAS TO BE TRUE

**Load-bearing:** that *drafting* is the binding constraint in a freelance designer's sales process. It isn't. A designer with pipeline duplicates last month's proposal in Google Docs; a designer without pipeline has nothing to draft. You're selling a vitamin to the wrong end of the funnel — and the ones who *do* pitch 6+ proposals a month are small agencies, who happily pay $50–200/mo. That's a different, better ICP than the one you named.

Supporting, ranked by blast radius:
1. **That "AI writes it" is differentiation in mid-2026.** [Upwork's Uma](https://www.upwork.com/uma) drafts proposals free in-product; HoneyBook ships AI proposal drafting on Essentials [T1, pricing pages]; ChatGPT is already installed. Your differentiator is a feature of three products your buyer already has.
2. **That $29/mo clears the "just use Bonsai" test.** Bonsai Essentials is $25/user/mo with proposals + contracts + invoicing [T1]; HoneyBook Starter is $29/mo annual with Smart Files [T1]. You're at parity price for a fraction of the surface area.
3. **That churn behaves.** Prosumer benchmark is ~5%/month logo churn [T2]. At $348 ACV that's a ~20-month life, ~$465 gross LTV, so CAC must stay under ~$155 — and freelance designers churn *harder* than the benchmark because their own revenue is lumpy and the tool is only valuable in months they're pitching.

### WHERE IT BREAKS

**Competing against free and against bundled.** In four searches I found Better Proposals ($12.25–$24.75), Proposify (from $19), Qwilr ($35/user), ProposalDraft ($19), Bonsai, HoneyBook, Moxie, Plutio, Agiled, Upwex, Proposal Genie, Propovo, BidPilotPro, UpCat, OhSweet — plus Upwork's free Uma [all T1/T3, pricing pages and comparison posts, July 2026]. *Defused by:* owning an outcome nobody else measures (win rate, project value), not a feature. *Cheapest test:* $300 of ads against "AI design proposal" — if CPC is above $4, the category is being farmed by people with better unit economics than you. *Cost of being wrong:* 6 months building a commodity.

**The treadmill.** $1M ARR at $348 = ~2,900 subs; at 5% monthly churn you need ~145 net-new every month *forever* to stand still. And the SEO for every "design proposal template" query is owned by Better Proposals, Bonsai, HoneyBook, Venngage and Dribbble with a decade of domain authority. *Defused by:* a channel they can't copy — a design community you already belong to, or embedding where the deal starts (Figma, Calendly, the discovery call). *Cost of being wrong:* a product that works and a business that doesn't.

**Motion/math check:** $348 ACV means self-serve, no demos, no onboarding calls, no human support. That part is at least internally consistent — you just don't have a channel yet, and at this ACV the channel *is* the business.

### THE MOTION (reshaped)

- **ICP:** US/UK independent designers and 2–5 person design studios billing $80K+/yr, doing project work (not retainer, not platform-hourly), who send ≥4 proposals/month. Countable via Dribbble/Contra pro profiles, Clutch small-studio listings, and design-community membership rolls. Rough N: 15K–40K [ASSUMPTION] — 8× smaller than the population you named, 5× more likely to pay, 3× the price tolerance.
- **Wedge:** discovery-call transcript → scoped, three-tier, priced proposal in 5 minutes. The unlock is *pricing confidence*, not prose.
- **Channel:** one design community you're already credible in, plus win-rate benchmark content that only you can publish because only you have the data.
- **Price:** $79–$99/mo, or $49 + 1% of accepted project value. Not $29. $29 anchors you against Bonsai and forfeits the outcome story.

### KILL CRITERIA

- Of 20 ICP designers interviewed in 30 days: if **fewer than 6** name proposal creation unprompted in their top-3 business frustrations — **kill the writing product**, and check whether lead-gen is the real business.
- If **fewer than 8 of 20** can state their proposal win rate from memory, they don't manage this as a metric — **the pricing-intelligence pitch has no buyer** and RESHAPE becomes KILL.
- Landing page + $500 ads, 45 days: **under 40 email signups or under 3% visitor→signup** — the message doesn't beat free.
- 20 hand-recruited free trials: **fewer than 8 still active in week 4** — no habit, so no subscription.
- 90 days post-launch: **monthly logo churn above 8%** — the leaky bucket wins; no channel outruns it at $348.

### NEXT 14 DAYS

1. **Interview 20 ICP designers**, not 20 freelancers. ~$1,000 in incentives. Ask what they did the last time they sent a proposal and what they charged — never "would you use this." Read-out: the 6-of-20 threshold above.
2. **Buy $300 of search ads** on "design proposal template" / "AI proposal designer." Read-out: CPC under $4 and ≥3% CTR, else the category is farmed.
3. **Pay for one month of Bonsai and one of HoneyBook** (~$54 total) and write a proposal in each. If you can't name three things you'd do materially better, you don't have a product.
4. **Price-test $29 vs $79 vs $49+1%** on the landing page, three variants. Read-out: if $79 converts at ≥60% of $29's rate, take the $79 — you were leaving 2.7× on the table.
